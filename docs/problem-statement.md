# Problem Statement

Ideally, a small or medium-sized factory would know at every moment whether each of its machines is running, idle, or developing a fault, and would keep this record without interruption, even when its network connection fails. With this knowledge, managers could plan maintenance in advance, reduce unplanned stoppages, and make production decisions from facts instead of guesses.

In reality, most legacy machines in small and medium enterprises cannot gather data, so the condition of a machine is judged by operators and recorded by hand, if recorded at all. Where digital monitoring does exist, it often depends on a working network: in one apparel case study, a short Wi-Fi drop stopped real-time tracking. Meanwhile, high initial and maintenance costs and a lack of knowledge are the main barriers to adopting new technology in Sri Lanka's apparel industry, which leaves small factories without an affordable way to monitor their machines.

As a result, machine stoppages go unrecorded, faults are noticed only after a breakdown, and owners cannot see where production time and money are being lost. This matters at a national level because small and medium enterprises make up 75% of active enterprises in Sri Lanka, yet they lag significantly behind large firms in adopting digital technology.
